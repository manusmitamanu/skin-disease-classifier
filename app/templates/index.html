How to read it

  • Red and yellow: the program looked here the most.

About this tool

A computer program that looks at a skin image and guesses which of 7 types it looks most like. It was made for learning. It is not a medical device.

It tries to refuse images that look very different from its training images. This is not perfect, so it can still be wrong on photos that look similar.

What it was taught with

  • About 10,000 dermatoscope images from a public collection called HAM10000
  • Dermatoscope images are close-ups taken with a special magnifier and light

How well it did on images it had not seen

  • About 77 out of 100 were labelled correctly
  • About 6 out of 10 melanomas were flagged. About 4 out of 10 were missed
  • When it flagged melanoma, only about 1 in 3 flags was a real melanoma

What the melanoma alert means

  • The program gives a chance (in %) that the image is a melanoma
  • We raise an alert when that chance is 25% or more
  • We kept the line low on purpose so fewer melanomas are missed. The cost is more false alarms
  • Below the line does NOT mean you are safe

LIMITATIONS

  • Phone photos and pictures from the internet. It will often be wrong
  • It tries to refuse images that are not dermatoscope-style, but this check is imperfect. It can wrongly refuse valid images and wrongly accept some invalid ones
  • Rare types, such as vascular lesions and dermatofibroma, because it saw few examples of them
  • It was tested only on one set of images, so results on other images may be worse
